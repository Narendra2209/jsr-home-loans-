Features & benefits

Why a home loan is the cheapest money you will ever borrow

01

The lowest rate in retail lending

Because the property secures the loan, home loans are priced 4–8 percentage points below personal loans. If you need money for anything the property can justify, this is the cheapest route.

02

No prepayment penalty

On floating-rate loans to individuals, lenders cannot charge you for paying early. Every bonus you put into the loan removes interest permanently.

03

Tenure up to 30 years

Long tenure keeps the EMI within reach when you are starting out. You can always shorten it later by prepaying, which is the sensible way to use it.

04

Tax deductions

Under the old tax regime, up to ₹2 lakh of interest under Section 24(b) and ₹1.5 lakh of principal under Section 80C. The new regime does not allow these for a self-occupied home — check which regime suits you.

05

Joint applications

Clubbing a spouse’s income raises eligibility sharply, and where each co-owner is also a co-borrower, each can claim the deductions separately under the old regime.

06

Concessions for women

Several lenders price loans 0.05%–0.10% lower where a woman is the primary applicant or co-owner, and Telangana’s registration charges also favour it in some cases.

07

A free legal check on your property

The lender’s legal and technical appraisal is, in effect, a second opinion on the title and the valuation. Many buyers discover a problem here that would have cost them far more later.

08

Balance transfer keeps lenders honest

You are never locked in. If your bank’s rate drifts above the market, you can move — which is exactly the leverage you use to get it reset.

Documents required

The complete checklist

Bring these once and we will handle the rest. Every applicant and co-applicant needs their own KYC and income set.

Identity & address (all applicants)

  • PAN card — mandatory, no substitute
  • Aadhaar card
  • Passport, voter ID or driving licence
  • Recent utility bill or rent agreement for current address
  • Passport-size photographs (3–4 each)

Income — salaried

  • Last 3 months’ salary slips
  • Form 16 for the last 2 years
  • ITR / acknowledgement for the last 2 years
  • 6 months’ bank statement of the salary account
  • Appointment or employment continuity letter
  • Proof of any variable pay or bonus you want counted

Income — self-employed

  • ITR with computation of income, last 3 years
  • Audited balance sheet and P&L with schedules, last 3 years
  • 12 months’ current and savings account statements
  • GST returns for the last 12 months, where registered
  • Business proof: Udyam, GST certificate, trade licence, partnership deed or MOA/AOA
  • Professional degree certificate for doctors, CAs, architects

Property documents

  • Sale agreement or allotment letter
  • Prior title chain — typically 13 to 30 years of linked deeds
  • Approved building plan and layout approval (HMDA / GHMC / DTCP as applicable)
  • Encumbrance certificate
  • Latest property tax receipt and, where relevant, the pattadar passbook
  • Occupancy certificate for completed buildings
  • NOC from the builder or society
  • Receipts for the own-contribution amount already paid

Existing loans

  • Sanction letters and repayment schedules for every running loan
  • 12 months’ statement of each loan account
  • Closure or no-dues certificates for loans closed in the last 2 years
  • Latest credit card statements

NRI applicants — additional

  • Valid passport with visa or work permit
  • Overseas employment contract, English translation if required
  • 3–6 months’ overseas salary slips
  • 6–12 months’ overseas bank statements, plus NRE/NRO statements
  • Overseas credit report where the lender asks for one
  • Notarised or apostilled Power of Attorney

Two things that hold files up more than anything else

A broken title chain. If a link deed from twenty years ago is missing, the lender’s advocate will stop the file. Ask the seller for the full chain before you pay an advance.

Own-contribution proof. Lenders release their share only after yours has gone in, and they want it traced through your bank account. Cash payments you cannot evidence do not count.

FAQs

Home loan questions

Almost all Indian home loans are floating, and for good reason: floating loans carry no prepayment penalty for individuals, and fixed offers are usually fixed only for the first few years before reverting. Unless you have a specific reason to lock the rate, floating with the freedom to prepay is the better deal.

Banks link floating retail loans to an external benchmark — usually the RBI repo rate. Your rate is that benchmark plus a spread fixed for you at sanction. When the repo rate moves, your rate moves with it. Two borrowers at the same bank can pay different rates because their spreads differ, which is why an older loan drifts above the market and why a spread reset is worth asking for.

Property insurance is usually required and is inexpensive. A loan-protection life policy is not legally compulsory, though it is often bundled.
