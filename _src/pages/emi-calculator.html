How the EMI is calculated

Every lender uses the same standard formula for an equated monthly instalment on a reducing-balance loan:

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

  • P is the principal — the amount you actually borrow, not the property price.
  • r is the monthly interest rate: the annual rate divided by 12, then by 100. At 8.5% a year, r = 0.0070833.
  • n is the number of monthly instalments — 20 years is 240.

Three things follow from that formula, and they are worth internalising before you sign anything:

  1. A longer tenure lowers the EMI but raises the total cost sharply. Stretching ₹50 lakh at 8.5% from 20 to 30 years drops the EMI by about ₹4,950 but adds roughly ₹34 lakh of interest.
  2. Early EMIs are almost all interest. On a 20-year loan at 8.5%, about 82% of your first instalment is interest. Prepayments made in years 1–5 therefore remove far more interest than the same amount paid in year 15.
  3. Small rate differences compound. Half a percentage point on a ₹50 lakh, 20-year loan is nearly ₹3.9 lakh. This is the entire reason to compare lenders rather than walk into the nearest branch.

One caveat on the arithmetic: most Indian lenders now charge interest on a daily reducing balance and round the EMI to the nearest rupee, and some collect a part-month “pre-EMI” interest between disbursement and your first full instalment. Those adjustments move the real figure by a few hundred rupees at most, but they are why the sanction letter never matches a calculator to the last rupee.
