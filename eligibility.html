Behind the number

The five things a lender checks

01

Repayment capacity (FOIR)

Your total EMIs, including the new one, are capped at a share of net income — roughly 45% at lower incomes rising to about 65% at higher ones. This is usually the binding constraint.

02

Credit score and history

750+ gets the best pricing. Lenders also look at how many enquiries you have made recently, any 30-day-plus delays, and settled or written-off accounts — a settlement hurts more than a late payment.

03

Age and remaining working life

The loan must normally end by age 60 for salaried applicants and 65–70 for self-employed. A 50-year-old salaried applicant is capped near 10 years of tenure, which cuts eligibility hard.

04

Property and loan-to-value

Funding is capped at 90% of value up to ₹30 lakh, 80% from ₹30–75 lakh and 75% above that. Approved plans, clear title and a valuation that supports the price all matter.

05

Income stability

Salaried applicants need continuous employment, usually 2+ years total with 6+ months in the current job. Self-employed applicants need 3 years of business vintage and consistent ITRs.

06

Co-applicants

Adding an earning spouse, parent or child clubs the incomes and can lift eligibility by 50–80%. Women co-owners also get a small rate concession from several lenders.

Eligibility criteria

What each lender expects to see

Who qualifies

  • Age: 21 to 60 years at loan maturity (some lenders allow 65 with a co-applicant).
  • Income: typically ₹25,000 net per month and above; the floor is higher in metros.
  • Work history: 2+ years of total employment, 6+ months in the current organisation.
  • Employer: listed, government, PSU and large private employers get better pricing.
  • Credit score: 700 minimum in practice, 750+ for the best rate.
  • Nationality: resident Indian.

Documents to keep ready

  • PAN card and Aadhaar
  • Latest 3 months’ salary slips
  • Form 16 and ITR for the last 2 years
  • 6 months’ bank statements of the salary account
  • Employment / appointment letter, or an ID card
  • Passport-size photographs of all applicants
  • Property agreement, approved plan and prior title chain
  • Own-contribution proof, and receipts for any payment already made

Who qualifies

  • Age: 23 to 65 years at loan maturity (70 with some housing finance companies).
  • Business vintage: at least 3 years in the same line of business.
  • Income: assessed on the last 2–3 years of ITRs; declining profits are the most common reason a good file is declined.
  • Banking: clean current account conduct, no cheque returns, turnover consistent with the declared income.
  • Credit score: 700 minimum, 750+ preferred.
  • Note: several lenders offer a banking-surrogate or GST-based programme when ITR income understates the real cash flow — ask us about it.

Documents to keep ready

  • PAN card and Aadhaar
  • ITR with computation of income for the last 3 years
  • Audited balance sheet and P&L for 3 years, with schedules
  • 12 months’ bank statements — current and savings
  • GST returns for the last 12 months, where registered
  • Business proof: Udyam, shop & establishment, trade licence or partnership deed
  • Professional qualification certificate, for doctors, CAs, architects
  • Property agreement, approved plan and prior title chain

Who qualifies

  • Status: NRI or Person of Indian Origin holding a valid passport and visa or work permit.
  • Age: 21 to 60 years at loan maturity; tenure is usually capped shorter than for residents, often 15–20 years.
  • Employment: at least 6 months to 1 year in the current overseas job, and often 2 years of overall overseas experience.
  • Funding: generally up to 75–80% of property value; repayment must come through NRE or NRO accounts by normal banking channels.
  • Power of Attorney: most lenders need a POA holder in India, which we can help you set up.

Documents to keep ready

  • Passport with valid visa or work permit, and PAN
  • Overseas employment contract, translated into English if needed
  • Latest 3–6 months’ overseas salary slips
  • 6–12 months’ overseas bank statements plus NRE/NRO account statements
  • Overseas credit report, where the lender asks for it
  • Overseas tax return or equivalent income proof
  • Notarised / apostilled Power of Attorney in favour of your representative in India
  • Property agreement, approved plan and prior title chain

Lists are typical across lenders and are not exhaustive. The exact document set depends on the lender and the property — we give you one consolidated checklist once we know which lender fits your case.

If the number is short

Six ways to raise your eligibility

  1. Add a co-applicant. An earning spouse, parent or adult child lets the lender club incomes. This is the single biggest lever available to most applicants.
  2. Close a small loan. Clearing a ₹12,000-a-month car loan can add roughly ₹13.8 lakh of home loan eligibility at 8.5% over 20 years. Closing the smallest EMI first usually beats part-paying the largest.
  3. Stretch the tenure. Going from 15 to 25 years increases eligibility by around 22% — but remember it also increases total interest, so treat it as a way to qualify, not a target.
  4. Declare all income. Rental income, an annual bonus with a two-year track record, and variable pay are counted by many lenders if you can evidence them.
  5. Fix your credit report first. Disputing a wrongly reported default or clearing a settled account can move you a rate band — see our CIBIL guide.
  6. Choose the right lender. FOIR caps, income multiples and treatment of variable pay differ meaningfully between banks. The same file can be worth ₹10 lakh more at one lender than another.
