Mortgage loan / LAP

Property you already own is the cheapest collateral you have

A loan against property lets you raise a large sum — for business expansion, a child’s education abroad, a medical emergency, debt consolidation or a second property — at a rate far below any unsecured loan, with tenure up to 15 years.

The property stays yours and you continue to live in it or earn rent from it. The lender simply holds a mortgage over it until the loan closes. What changes is the cost of your money: LAP is typically priced 1–2.5 percentage points above a home loan, and 5–9 points below a personal loan.

Property eligibility

Which properties lenders will fund

The property matters more than it does in a home loan — it is the security. Two applicants with identical incomes can get very different offers because of what they are mortgaging.

Usually accepted

  • Self-occupied residential — flat or independent house with clear title. Best LTV and rate.
  • Rented residential — rental income can often be added to your eligibility.
  • Commercial — shops, offices, showrooms in an established location. LTV usually a notch lower.
  • Industrial — sheds and units in approved industrial areas, with fewer lenders and a lower LTV.
  • Residential plots — open plots in approved layouts, typically at 40%–50% LTV.
  • Under joint ownership — provided every co-owner joins as a co-applicant.

Difficult or declined

  • Agricultural land — almost universally excluded.
  • Unapproved layouts or deviations from the sanctioned plan — a common reason a Hyderabad file stalls.
  • Gram panchayat properties outside a recognised municipal limit — only a few lenders, at low LTV.
  • GPA-based holdings without a registered sale deed.
  • Disputed or litigated title, or a broken chain of prior deeds.
  • Property let to a tenant with protected tenancy, or occupied by someone other than the owner without documentation.
  • Very old construction with little residual life, or a property whose remaining life is shorter than the tenure.

Two things people get wrong about LAP

The valuation, not the asking price. Your eligibility is a percentage of the lender’s valuer’s number. In practice that comes in 10%–20% below what you believe the property is worth.

Income still has to service the EMI. A high-value property does not by itself get you a large loan. The lender applies the same FOIR test as any other loan, so the smaller of the two limits — property value or repayment capacity — is what you get.

Documents required

What to keep ready

KYC — every owner and co-applicant

  • PAN card and Aadhaar
  • Passport, voter ID or driving licence
  • Current address proof
  • Passport-size photographs

Income — salaried

  • 3 months’ salary slips
  • Form 16 and ITR, last 2 years
  • 6 months’ salary account statement

Income — self-employed

  • ITR with computation, last 3 years
  • Audited financials with schedules, last 3 years
  • 12 months’ current account statements
  • GST returns, last 12 months
  • Business registration proof

Property — the critical set

  • Registered sale deed in the applicant’s name
  • Prior title chain — linked documents going back 13–30 years
  • Encumbrance certificate for the same period
  • Approved plan and layout approval
  • Latest property tax receipt and, for land, the pattadar passbook
  • Occupancy certificate, where applicable
  • Existing loan statement if the property is already mortgaged
  • Registered lease agreement if the property is let out

End-use declaration

  • A written statement of purpose — business expansion, education, medical, debt consolidation
  • Supporting quotes or invoices where the lender asks for them
  • For debt consolidation: statements of the loans being cleared

If the property is jointly held

  • Every co-owner must join as a co-applicant, whether or not they earn
  • Consent and KYC from each co-owner
  • Succession or legal heir certificate where an owner has passed away
  • Registered release deed where a share has already been relinquished
